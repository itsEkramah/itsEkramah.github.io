# 05 — Content, Editorial Standards, and Evidence

## Editorial voice
A real scientist/developer writing in first person. Clear, specific, precise, thoughtful; not promotional hype. Avoid "passionate", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge solutions", "leverage", "synergy", boastful SEO promises, repetitive mechanical intros, unearned authority, fabricated claims and vague conclusions.

## Known biographical data — MUST VERIFY before public publication
User reports BS Bioinformatics, Quaid-i-Azam University; NIH internship with AMR surveillance R analytics and laboratory exposure; work on genomic AMR machine learning, PathoScope-AI prototype and research projects; programming interests Python, R, ML, bioinformatics. Verify exact credentials, dates, results, project ownership, confidentiality, GitHub repository links, demo URLs and public CV directly with files or user-approved sources. Do not assume every prior project is public or production-grade. Never silently add a thesis, company client, ranking result or publication.

## About/Experience/Portfolio disclosure
- Indicate student/intern/independent work accurately; distinguish employment, internship, coursework, and personal projects.
- Methodological caution: model performance numbers must have data/method context; disclose data leakage risks and limitations. Never invent measured metrics.
- For AMR surveillance, use openly licensed datasets or properly de-identified, permissioned material; do not publish patient identifiers or internal surveillance reports.
- PathoScope-AI: prototype status, actual feature scope, demo link only if verified.
- Cite external papers, figures and code licenses.

## Article writing standards
Each post: specific reader problem, introduction that answers intent, methods (when applicable), prerequisites/environment, reproducible example, version info, limitations, references/citations, related work. No blind AI-written filler. Where unsupported, leave a draft unpublished.

## Article taxonomy
- Bioinformatics & Genomics: NGS, RNA-seq, genomics, ML validation, biological workflows.
- Medicine & Pharma: AMR, clinical data methods, drug discovery, medical analysis, pharma informatics.
- Data & Analytics: Python, R, SQL, statistics, dashboards, automation.
- Software & Web: FastAPI, Django, APIs, web dev, deployment and debugging.
- Business & SEO: technical SEO, indexing, schema, AI-search experiments, credible business workflows.
`format`: guide / tutorial / research note / review / opinion / case study, rather than extra category clutter.

## Initial publishing plan
Launch with accurately sourced personal/professional pages and 0–3 completed, verified articles. It is better to publish 1 strong piece than 12 invented placeholders. Do not show empty article cards or fake publish dates. Queue draft article ideas internally, excluded from site and sitemap.

## Suggested article candidates (IDEAS ONLY)
- Leakage-safe validation for genomic AMR classification.
- Practical antimicrobial resistance analysis with R on a public dataset.
- Reproducible Python data-cleaning reports.
- How to organize static articles for crawlability and maintainability.
- FastAPI data workflow tutorial only once a tested example exists.

## SEO experiment ethics
Document baseline dates, target queries, Search Console country/device/date ranges, impressions, clicks and average position. Distinguish correlation from causal impact; do not call rank changes caused by writing alone or claim first-page placement without evidence. Search visibility ≠ freelance-client conversions.

## Review gates
Author review for biography, methods review for research claims, security/privacy review for data, link verification, editorial proofreading, publish date and change note. Content owners must approve public-facing facts.
